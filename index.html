05 · Case study

Agentic AI Platform
at United Airlines.

What it took to move a multi-agent system from a lab demo to a production platform serving 150+ internal users across engineering and operations.

TL;DR

A production multi-agent AI platform for internal operations at United Airlines: 150+ users, 73% less manual review (roughly 300 hours per month saved), 2.5× task completion, and +4 points internal NPS.

The problem

Internal operations and engineering teams at United were losing days a month to repetitive investigative work: chasing information across siloed systems, hand-triaging reviews, and repeating the same reasoning steps across similar cases. Existing point tools solved narrow pieces of the workflow; nothing composed them end to end. Everyone knew where the bottleneck was; nobody had built the plumbing to remove it.

The approach

I designed and owned a production-grade agentic platform layered on top of United's enterprise LLM SDK. Three subsystems, one product:

  1. Multi-agent orchestration. A planner and tool-using agents with structured hand-offs, so a single request could safely fan out to multiple specialists, gather evidence, and produce a decision.
  2. RAG substrate. 100K+ documents indexed with retrieval and prompt orchestration tuned to the operations corpus, so agents reasoned over ground truth instead of hallucinating from priors.
  3. Evaluation and feedback loop. Automated ground-truth generation and scoring, plus a monitoring pipeline that let us catch regressions and iterate on prompts and tools without breaking production.

Tradeoffs I chose

  • Latency versus reasoning depth. Multi-agent workflows are slower than a single-shot call. I parallelized tool execution and cached retrievals; users trade a few seconds of wait for a 2.5× lift in what actually gets completed.
  • Response speed versus RAG accuracy. Bigger retrievals help recall but hurt latency and cost. I tuned retrieval and prompt orchestration per workflow rather than one-size-fits-all, and issue resolution got 47% faster without sacrificing grounding.
  • Governance overhead versus iteration velocity. Automated ground truth and scoring meant continuous evaluation did not slow the team down. It caught quality regressions before users did.

Result

Adopted across engineering and operations teams, with 150+ users at last count. Manual review time dropped 73% (roughly 300 hours per month saved), task completion rose 2.5×, and internal NPS improved by four points on the workflows the platform touched. Integration into internal platforms meant adoption spread without top-down mandates. The tool caught on because it worked.

What I would generalize

The platform pattern held: evaluation infrastructure is not a nice-to-have; it is the substrate that lets you keep shipping. Multi-agent designs earn their complexity only when you can ground them and measure them; without both, they collapse into unreliable demos. That is the lesson I would carry into any team building agentic AI at the customer edge.
